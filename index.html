About This Portfolio

What I Built This Quarter

This portfolio documents a quarter-long investigation into how seven real companies — from a drug discovery startup to a self-driving car company — actually deploy AI at scale. In each lab I moved beyond conceptual understanding to build working systems: a Spotify recommendation engine in Python, a Netflix A/B testing pipeline in Google Colab, and a clinical insurance appeal agent in n8n, among others. What I learned most wasn't the mechanics of any single model — it was the gap between AI hype and implementation reality: the data pipelines that break, the governance frameworks that get skipped, and the equity questions that rarely appear in press releases. Each lab is published as a standalone GitHub Pages project linked below.

Built two AI agents in n8n — an AI Insurance Advocate that drafts appeal letters for wrongly denied insurance claims, and a personal event planner — both powered by Claude Sonnet via the Anthropic API. Key Takeaways

What the Quarter Taught Me

01

The Pipeline Is the Product

In every lab, the model was the least interesting part. The real engineering challenge was the data pipeline feeding it — cleaning, labeling, versioning, and monitoring the inputs. A state-of-the-art model on stale or biased data is worse than a simple model on clean data.

02

Governance Can't Be Retrofitted

Every system I studied — from Waymo's edge-case detection to the Insurance Advocate's hallucination guardrails — worked because governance was designed in from the start, not bolted on after deployment. Accountability, audit trails, and human oversight checkpoints need to be architectural decisions, not afterthoughts.

03

Business Value Lives in Integration

Model accuracy is a threshold requirement, not a differentiator. What separates Spotify's recommendation engine from a research paper is the workflow integration — how the model's output flows into the product, how it's monitored in production, and how it adapts when user behavior shifts. The last mile is where value is created or lost.

04

Equity Is a Deployment Decision

Who gets access to AI tools shapes who benefits from them. The Insurance Advocate lab made this concrete: if only large, well-resourced hospitals can afford the tool, insurers quietly shift denial patterns toward rural clinics that can't fight back. Every deployment decision is an equity decision — and it needs to be treated as one from day one.
